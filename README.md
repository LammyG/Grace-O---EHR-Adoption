# Grace-O---EHR-Adoption
# Experiences of Healthcare Professionals on the Adoption of Electronic Health Records in Low-Income and Developing Countries

A systematic review of qualitative evidence on how healthcare professionals experience the adoption and implementation of Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems across low-income and developing countries.

Prepared as part of the **PMIM701** module assignment (MSc programme).

## Authors

- Grace Odubanjo
- Olamide Okunola

## Overview

This repository contains the full working materials for a two-part systematic review project:

- **Part A — Narrative Literature Review**: scopes the background literature on EHR adoption, barriers, facilitators, and outcomes in low-resource health systems.
- **Part B — Systematic Review (Publication-Ready Paper)**: a full PRISMA-guided systematic review synthesising qualitative evidence from healthcare professionals' first-hand experiences of EHR adoption.

## Research Question

What are healthcare professionals' experiences of adopting Electronic Health Records (EHRs) in low-income and developing countries?

## Methodology

- **Framework**: SPIDER (Sample, Phenomenon of Interest, Design, Evaluation, Research type), used in preference to PICO as this review addresses subjective experience rather than a measurable clinical outcome (Cooke et al., 2012).
- **Databases searched**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science.
- **Screening tool**: [Rayyan](https://www.rayyan.ai) (Ouzzani et al., 2016), used for deduplication and blinded title/abstract screening.
- **Quality appraisal**: CASP Qualitative Checklist (10-item), minimum inclusion threshold of 7/10.
- **Synthesis approach**: Thematic synthesis (Thomas & Harden, 2008).

## Included Studies

10 qualitative studies across 7 countries: Ghana, South Africa, Tanzania, Botswana, Morocco, and Iran.

## Key Themes

1. Infrastructural and technical barriers
2. Human resource and training deficits
3. Organisational and leadership challenges
4. System design, usability, and interoperability
5. Perceived benefits and positive experiences

A cross-cutting finding of this review is the coexistence of genuine perceived benefit and persistent structural constraint within the same participants and facilities, rather than a simple success/failure narrative.
